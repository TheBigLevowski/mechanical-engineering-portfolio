The Hardy Cross Calculator

Built a macro free Excel solver to explore how Darcy Weisbach and Hazen Williams predict flow and pressure in a 10 pipe, 3 loop cast iron water network. After 8 iterations per loop, the models' converged flow predictions agreed within approximately 2%, but revealed a major pressure difference in 1 branch.

Numerical method

Balancing Flow Through Each Loop

Continuity alone cannot determine every pipe flow in a looped network. The solver starts with estimated flows that satisfy continuity, calculates the signed head loss imbalance around each loop, and adjusts the flow in every pipe in that loop:

ΔQ = − Σh / Σ( n · h / |q| )

The exponent n equals 2 for Darcy Weisbach and 1.852 for Hazen Williams. The workbook shows 8 iterations for each method, keeping every flow correction visible for review and comparison.

Global inputs
Keeps gravity, kinematic viscosity, cast iron roughness ε, and the Hazen Williams coefficient C together so input changes update the full model
Pipe properties
Sets each pipe's length, diameter, area, roughness, and From → To reference direction
Loop definition
Tracks which pipes belong to each loop and their traversal signs in a matrix to reduce manual sign errors
Calculated output
Reports each pipe's converged flow for both friction models and calculates outlet pressure along 3 defined paths

How the Friction Model Changes Predicted Pressure

Starting from a 100 psi inlet, total head loss along A→B→C reaches 175.2 ft under Darcy Weisbach and 64.5 ft under Hazen Williams. That difference changes the predicted outlet condition: Hazen Williams gives +28.8 psi at node C, while Darcy Weisbach predicts −19.2 psi, indicating that the specified inlet pressure cannot support the required positive outlet pressure.

Traced the difference to pipe BC: 4,000 ft of 4 inch cast iron pipe carrying 0.52 cfs at approximately 6 ft/s. The upstream 20 inch pipe adds only several feet of head loss, making the resistance of this 1 branch decisive in the pressure assessment.
