The SMART Hawk · Structural test system

The Bird Stand

Designed to support The SMART Hawk at its center of gravity, The Bird Stand constrains translation while allowing pitch and roll through a ball and socket joint. The system enables repeatable control testing under laboratory airflow conditions.

Test limitation

Replacing vehicle based testing with controlled laboratory evaluation

Initial control testing supported The SMART Hawk on a ball and socket fixture above a moving truck bed. The method did not produce sustained stabilization because atmospheric gusts, vehicle induced upwash, and nonuniform local velocity prevented repeatable input conditions.

Converted the field test concept into a rigid indoor platform that constrains translation, preserves pitch and roll freedom, and interfaces with controlled airflow from The Wind Generator.

Mechanical architecture

Rigid load path with controlled rotational freedom

Minimized fixture compliance so measured aircraft motion represents aerodynamic and control response rather than deformation of the supporting structure.

Base structure
Combined a ½ inch steel reinforced base with aluminum and steel supports to achieve negligible deflection under test load
Aircraft joint
Located the ball and socket interface at the aircraft's center of gravity, decoupling rotational response from translational motion
Applied loads
Accommodates simultaneous aerodynamic loading from The Wind Generator and propulsive loading from the aircraft
Test objective
Supports repeatable flight control calibration under reproducible laboratory inflow conditions
Validated capabilities
  • Constrained motion testing — supports the aircraft in front of The Wind Generator while maintaining negligible fixture deflection under load.
  • Center of gravity measurement — uses the same frame to locate and adjust aircraft balance, improving available tail pitch authority before flight.
Planned capability
  • Integrate 4 5 kg load cells to resolve lift and drag directly through the existing structural platform.
  • Conduct closed loop stability calibration after The Wind Generator's airflow feedback system is commissioned.

The modular frame accepts additional instruments without requiring a separate support structure.

Engineering contribution

Conceived the test approach and led the design and fabrication of both stand versions.

V1 used a primarily wooden structure to establish the test method during airflow and flutter trials. It verified that supporting the aircraft at its center of gravity could isolate pitch and roll response while constraining translation.

V2 introduced a steel reinforced base and additional aluminum and steel supports, reducing structural deflection to negligible levels under test load.
