The SMART Hawk

Built around the way a hawk changes shape in flight, The SMART Hawk is a 1.67 kg UAV with a 1.2 m span and 5 active morphing degrees of freedom. Independently controlled wings tuck asymmetrically, and a 9 feather tail adjusts pitch, roll, and spread in place of conventional ailerons, elevator, and rudder.

Design objective

Turning a Hawk's Flight Mechanics Into an Aircraft

Red tailed hawks reshape their wings and tail as they move between efficient soaring and rapid dives.

The SMART Hawk brings that motion into its flight controls. The design challenge was to keep the feathered joints free to move while controlling flutter and keeping the structure light.

Combined an articulated skeleton inspired by a hawk with prepreg carbon fiber feathers molded from scanned hawk geometry. Elastic tendons retract the feathers and bring them together as the wings tuck.

How the aircraft changes shape

Controlling Flight Through 5 Active Degrees of Freedom

2 independent wing actuators and 3 tail actuators respond to roll, pitch, and yaw commands by changing the aircraft's shape.

Tucking Each Wing Independently

A 3D printed humerus, ulna, radius, and metacarpus mechanism moves the outer 28% of each wing using 1 servo. Tucking a wing reduces its area by 22.5%, creating the aerodynamic asymmetry intended to roll the aircraft toward that side.

Composite feathers
13 per wing — 2 prepreg carbon fiber plies formed around a pre bent aluminum spar
Passive retraction
Orthodontic elastics pull the free feathers inward, while fishing line limits how far they extend
Aerodynamic geometry
5° spanwise dihedral with tip washout to delay stall onset
Actuator margin
Simscape predicted 2.07 N at the metacarpus against 1.92 N total wing drag, indicating a force margin under the modeled aerodynamic load

Moving the Tail Across 3 Axes

Mounted 9 balsa and carbon feathers on a printed bracket with a 4 bar linkage for pitch, a carbon torque rod for roll, and a PTFE guided cable for spread. The tail's 487 cm² working area provided enough roll authority to bank the test aircraft without ailerons.

Pitch mechanism
A servo drives a 4 bar linkage, with the tail bracket acting as the rocker
Roll mechanism
6 mm carbon torque rod bonded to the pitch hinge
Spread mechanism
Crimped guitar wire moves both outer feathers symmetrically, and elastics pull them back

Computational analysis

Taking Hawk Geometry From Scan to Simulation

Used a section from a 3D scan of a red tailed hawk to define the feather airfoil. Analyzed the section in XFoil, sized the planform in MachUpX, and tested the complete feathered geometry through RANS CFD in ANSYS Fluent and OpenFOAM.

Compared morphing and conventional aircraft in MachUpX using matched wing and tail areas. The model predicted that asymmetric wing tucking produced more roll than traditional ailerons at every angle of attack above 2°, while the larger morphing tail produced greater pitch and yaw authority than the matched elevator and rudder surfaces. Adding 9° of sweep and −4° of tip twist raised the predicted stall angle from 11.5° to 12°.

Composite fabrication

Building 26 Wing Feathers With Consistent Camber

CFD predicted that flat feathers would not provide enough lift. Composite molds held the required camber through layup and cure for all 26 wing feathers.

Cutting Structural Parts for Consistent Assembly

Laser cut the inner wing ribs, stringers, spars, and 9 tail feathers from saved geometry files, using 2 mm balsa for the feathers. The files kept airfoil profiles consistent and made damaged parts straightforward to reproduce.

Built the 5° dihedral into the stringer geometry to avoid residual stress from assembly and improve symmetry between the wings, a requirement highlighted by flight testing.

Choosing Materials Through Testing and Structural Analysis

Tested ASTM D638 specimens in PLA, PLA Aero, ASA, and carbon filled PLA at 4 gyroid infill levels on a 10 kN tensile machine. For PLA and PLA CF, 15% infill outperformed 30%, guiding the choice of 15% gyroid for most printed parts. PLA CF was selected for loaded wing mechanisms and UV resistant ASA for the tail.

Checked components against a 1.5 factor of safety target using Brittle Coulomb Mohr for printed and wooden parts and Tsai Hill for composites. Predicted margins were 1.45 for the wing mechanism, 1.45 for the tail bracket, and 1.05 for the pitch hinge. Replaceable components made the airframe easier to repair after flight damage.

Structural analysis tool

The Tail Calculator

Built the Tail Calculator to check bending from the cantilevered 9 feather tail and torsion from the roll servo. It updates the analysis as tail mass, actuator torque, and rod geometry change, reducing repeated manual calculations.

The tool models the tail rod as a cantilever with an end load. Users can enter servo torque directly or calculate it from tangential force and moment arm, then find support reactions, maximum bending moment, total torsion, and combined stresses for the chosen rod diameter.

CSV exports and generated Arduino compatible constants carry the same results into project documentation and embedded code, keeping the analysis traceable.

Flight development

Testing 1 Morphing System at a Time

Added each morphing system to a proven baseline airframe so changes in flight behavior and failure modes could be traced to a specific design change.

Stage 01

Establishing the Baseline Aircraft

Built a foamboard, balsa, and PLA aircraft with conventional ailerons and elevator, matching The SMART Hawk's planform, area, and 5° dihedral. Stable flight established the baseline and placed the required center of gravity at 23% of chord behind the inner wing leading edge.

Stage 02

Testing the Morphing Tail

Replaced the baseline aircraft's conventional tail with the feathered morphing assembly. It delivered a comparable roll rate without ailerons and a higher pitch rate without a horizontal stabilizer, using 2 servos instead of 3. Average turn radius increased from 24.9 m to 30.0 m.

Stage 03

Testing the Morphing Wings

Fitted the morphing wings to an otherwise conventional airframe to test structural response and actuation without adding tail morphing as a 2nd variable. The tests demonstrated that the feathered wings could operate under aerodynamic load.

Stage 04

The SMART Hawk

Completed 2 flights of approximately 10 seconds with the full morphing aircraft. Flight 1 exposed feather movement under load caused by small build asymmetries, which were addressed with interlaced elastics and fishing line. Flight 2 maintained straight flight under morphing control before losing altitude during an attempted banked turn. A complete turn using wing tuck roll control remains unverified in flight.

Flight validation

What Flight Testing Has Demonstrated

The complete morphing aircraft demonstrated hand launch, climb, and straight flight across 2 flights of approximately 10 seconds. The video shows the 2nd flight through the start of an unsuccessful banked turn attempt.

Demonstrated in flight
  • Morphing tail control. Banked the test aircraft without ailerons, achieving a comparable roll rate and higher pitch rate with 2 servos instead of 3. Average turn radius was 30.0 m across 7 turns, compared with 24.9 m across 39 conventional turns.
  • Complete aircraft flight. Completed 2 flights of approximately 10 seconds, including straight flight using morphing controls.
  • In flight actuation. Operated the wing and tail mechanisms under aerodynamic load while the airframe carried cruise and maneuver loads.
Predicted, not yet demonstrated
  • Wing tuck roll control. Lifting line analysis and CFD predict more roll than conventional ailerons above 2° angle of attack, and Simscape predicts sufficient actuator force. A complete banked turn using this control method has not yet been demonstrated.
  • Closed loop stabilization. Testing on a moving truck did not achieve sustained stabilization because the temporary setup could not provide repeatable airflow.

The Wind Generator was built to provide the controlled full span airflow needed to investigate these remaining validation gaps.

Preparing Pilots for Morphing Flight Controls

Built a RealFlight Evolution model so pilots could practice before flying the physical aircraft. The model captures how the controls interact: tucking a wing shifts the center of gravity 2.1 in forward and 1.5 in away from the tucked side, while tail roll and pitch commands remain aerodynamically coupled.

The publication describes The SMART Hawk as the 1st model to combine cambered artificial feathers with a morphing tail that can control the aircraft independently of wing morphing.

Engineering contribution

Led testing for the 17 person SMART Hawk team, developing the equipment and procedures used to evaluate propulsion, structural response, and flight controls.

Built a laboratory testing approach around the Bird Stand, an expanded Wind Generator, and a rebuilt Thrust Stand. These systems brought critical control and propulsion decisions into repeatable ground tests.

Used the initial truck test to define requirements for the Bird Stand and Wind Generator after gusts, vehicle upwash, and uneven airflow prevented sustained stabilization.

Also helped build and prepare the aircraft through composite layup, laser cutting, mechanical assembly, wiring, and test setup.
