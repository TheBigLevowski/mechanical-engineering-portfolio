The SMART Hawk

The SMART Hawk is a 1.67 kg, 1.2 m span UAV that replaces ailerons, elevator, and rudder with 5 active morphing degrees of freedom. Independently actuated wings provide asymmetric tucking, while a 9 feather tail controls pitch, roll, and spread.

Design objective

Replicating avian control without conventional surfaces

Red tailed hawks transition between efficient soaring and rapid diving by continuously reshaping their wings and tail rather than deflecting discrete control surfaces.

The SMART Hawk applies that principle across the complete control architecture. The primary engineering tradeoff was maintaining jointed, feathered mobility while limiting flutter and structural mass.

Integrated an avian inspired articulated skeleton, prepreg carbon fiber feathers molded from scanned hawk geometry, and elastic tendon elements that retract and nest adjacent feathers.

Morphing control architecture

5 active degrees of freedom

2 independent wing actuators and 3 tail actuators convert roll, pitch, and yaw commands into geometric changes rather than conventional control surface deflections.

Asymmetric wing tucking

Articulated the outer 28% of each wing through a 3D printed humerus, ulna, radius, and metacarpus mechanism driven by 1 servo. Unilateral tucking reduces that wing's area by 22.5%, creating the aerodynamic asymmetry required to roll toward the tucked side.

Composite feathers
13 per wing — 2 prepreg carbon fiber plies formed around a pre bent aluminum spar
Passive retraction
Orthodontic elastics retract free feathers while fishing line constrains maximum extension
Aerodynamic geometry
5° spanwise dihedral with tip washout to delay stall onset
Actuator margin
Simscape predicted 2.07 N at the metacarpus against 1.92 N total wing drag, demonstrating available force margin under modeled aerodynamic load

3 axis tail morphing

Integrated 9 balsa and carbon feathers on a printed bracket using a 4 bar pitch linkage, carbon torque rod roll axis, and PTFE guided spread cable. The 487 cm² working area generated sufficient roll authority to bank the test aircraft without ailerons.

Pitch mechanism
Servo driven 4 bar linkage with the tail bracket functioning as the rocker
Roll mechanism
6 mm carbon torque rod bonded to the pitch hinge
Spread mechanism
Crimped guitar wire actuates both outer feathers symmetrically while elastics provide return force

Computational analysis

Airfoil and planform derived from biological geometry

Derived the feather airfoil from a section through a 3D scan of a red tailed hawk specimen. Characterized the section in XFoil, sized the planform in MachUpX, and evaluated the complete feathered geometry using RANS CFD in ANSYS Fluent and OpenFOAM.

These values are computational results from XFoil, MachUpX, and RANS CFD. Flight demonstrated and predicted capabilities are separated under Flight validation.

Compared morphing and conventional configurations in MachUpX at matched wing and tail area. The analysis predicted that asymmetric wing tucking generated more roll than traditional ailerons at every angle of attack above 2°, while the larger morphing tail generated greater pitch and yaw authority than matched elevator and rudder surfaces. Adding 9° of sweep and −4° of tip twist increased predicted stall onset from 11.5° to 12°.

Composite fabrication

Repeatable manufacture of 26 cambered wing feathers

CFD predicted insufficient lift from flat feather geometry. Composite tooling therefore preserved the required camber through layup and cure across all 26 wing feathers.

Repeatable laser cut primary structure

Laser cut the inner wing ribs, stringers, spars, and 9 2 mm balsa tail feathers from controlled geometry files. This process maintained consistent airfoil profiles and enabled damaged components to be reproduced without redesign.

Incorporated the 5° dihedral directly into the stringer geometry, eliminating assembly induced residual stress and improving left to right wing symmetry identified as critical during flight testing.

Material selection through tensile testing and FEA

Tested ASTM D638 specimens in PLA, PLA Aero, ASA, and carbon filled PLA across 4 gyroid infill levels using a 10 kN tensile machine. Because 15% infill outperformed 30% for PLA and PLA CF, selected 15% gyroid for most printed components; assigned PLA CF to loaded wing mechanisms and UV resistant ASA to the tail.

Evaluated components against a 1.5 factor of safety target using Brittle Coulomb Mohr for printed and wooden structures and Tsai Hill for composites. Predicted margins were 1.45 for the wing mechanism, 1.45 for the tail bracket, and 1.05 for the pitch hinge. Designed the airframe for component level replacement to support rapid recovery following flight damage.

Structural analysis tool

The Tail Calculator

Developed The Tail Calculator to evaluate combined bending from the 9 feather cantilevered mass and torsion from roll servo actuation, eliminating repeated manual calculations as tail mass, actuator torque, and rod geometry changed.

Models the tail rod as a cantilever with an end load, accepts direct servo torque or derives torque from tangential force and moment arm, and calculates support reactions, maximum bending moment, total torsion, and combined stresses for a specified rod diameter.

Exports complete results to CSV and generates Arduino compatible constants, maintaining traceability between structural analysis, project documentation, and embedded implementation.

Incremental flight development

Isolating 1 morphing system at each stage

Introduced each morphing subsystem on a validated baseline airframe, isolating variables so flight behavior and failure modes could be attributed to a specific design change.

Stage 01

Conventional baseline aircraft

Built a foamboard, balsa, and PLA baseline with conventional ailerons and elevator while matching The SMART Hawk's planform, area, and 5° dihedral. Stable flight established the control case and located the required center of gravity at 23% of chord aft of the inner wing leading edge.

Stage 02

Morphing tail validation

Replaced the conventional tail on the validated airframe with the feathered morphing assembly. The system achieved comparable roll rate without ailerons, increased pitch rate without a horizontal stabilizer, and reduced actuator count from 3 servos to 2; average turn radius increased from 24.9 m to 30.0 m.

Stage 03

Morphing wing validation

Installed the morphing wings on an otherwise conventional airframe to evaluate structural behavior and actuation without tail morphing as a 2nd variable. This stage demonstrated feathered wing operation under aerodynamic load.

Stage 04

The SMART Hawk

Completed 2 approximately 10 second flights with the full morphing configuration. Flight 1 identified load induced feather migration caused by small build asymmetries; interlaced elastics and fishing line corrected the condition. Flight 2 maintained straight flight under morphing control before losing altitude during an attempted banked turn. A completed turn using wing tuck roll control remains unverified in flight.

Flight validation

Separating demonstrated capability from prediction

Demonstrated hand launch, climb, and straight flight using the complete morphing configuration across 2 flights of approximately 10 seconds. The video documents the 2nd flight through the start of an unsuccessful banked turn attempt.

Demonstrated in flight
  • Morphing tail control. Achieved banked flight without ailerons at a comparable roll rate and higher pitch rate using 2 servos instead of 3; measured a 30.0 m average radius over 7 turns versus 24.9 m over 39 conventional turns.
  • Complete aircraft flight. Completed 2 approximately 10 second flights, including straight flight under morphing control.
  • In flight actuation. Demonstrated wing and tail mechanism operation under aerodynamic loading while the airframe sustained cruise and maneuver loads.
Predicted, not yet demonstrated
  • Wing tuck roll control. Lifting line and CFD predict greater roll generation than conventional ailerons above 2° angle of attack, while Simscape predicts adequate actuator force; a complete banked turn has not yet been demonstrated.
  • Closed loop stabilization. Truck mounted evaluation did not achieve sustained stabilization because the temporary fixture could not provide repeatable inflow.

Closing these validation gaps requires controlled full span inflow, which is the design purpose of The Wind Generator.

Pilot training for coupled morphing controls

Developed a RealFlight Evolution model to train pilots without risking the physical airframe. The model captures coupled behavior: unilateral wing tucking shifts the center of gravity 2.1 in forward and 1.5 in away from the tucked side, while tail roll and pitch commands remain aerodynamically coupled.

The publication identifies The SMART Hawk as the 1st model combining cambered artificial feathers with a morphing tail capable of controlling the aircraft independently of wing morphing.

Engineering contribution

Served as testing lead for the 17 person team developing The SMART Hawk, with responsibility for the equipment and procedures used to validate propulsion, structural response, and flight controls.

Established a laboratory test path for the aircraft by developing the constrained motion The Bird Stand, expanding The Wind Generator, and rebuilding The Thrust Stand. These systems moved critical control and propulsion decisions from trial and error flight testing into repeatable ground tests.

An initial truck mounted test did not achieve sustained stabilization because of gusts, vehicle upwash, and nonuniform flow. Converted those findings into requirements for The Bird Stand and The Wind Generator.

Also supported aircraft manufacturing through composite layup, laser cut structural parts, mechanical assembly, wiring, and test preparation.
