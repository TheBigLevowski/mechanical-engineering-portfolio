Propulsion selection

Turning Propulsion Requirements Into Tested Hardware

The SMART Hawk's aerodynamic model used an 11 m/s cruise condition. Testing compared available motors and propellers to assess thrust for the 1.67 kg airframe.

Tested 1100 KV and 1250 KV motors with multiple propellers. The selected 1250 KV motor and 11 × 5.5 inch propeller delivered 18 N of static thrust, giving the aircraft a thrust to weight ratio of 1.1.

Upgraded the 9th cohort's mechanical platform with corrected sensor wiring, more reliable communication, and a new browser interface for capturing test data.

Measuring thrust

Measuring Thrust Along the Motor Axis

Aligned the motor, load path, and load cell to measure axial thrust and reduce the influence of loads acting in other directions.

Mechanical structure
Combines a rigid base, vertical support, and forward facing motor mount with interchangeable hardware for comparing motor and propeller setups
Force measurement
Reads a 5 kg single axis load cell through an HX711 24 bit ADC, using ESP32 GPIO 4 for data and GPIO 2 for clock
Electrical integration
Matched sensor wiring to the firmware pinout and added a common ground to stabilize force readings without software remapping
Calibration method
Uses a known mass to calculate the calibration factor and saves it in nonvolatile memory so calibration remains available after a restart
Operating envelope
Measures static thrust within the load cell's rated capacity

Data acquisition interface

Live Thrust Data Through a Wireless Dashboard

Configured the ESP32 to host the dashboard from onboard memory through its own Wi Fi access point. Users can connect directly without a laboratory network or additional software.

The dashboard displays live and peak thrust in grams, newtons, and pounds force, with controls to start, pause, tare, and reset the peak reading. A fixed 0–5000 g plot makes startup, steady state, and shutdown behavior easy to compare.

Users can set the sample interval from 10 ms to 2000 ms, with a 50 ms default, and export each test as a timestamped CSV for further analysis.

Validated capabilities
  • Axial force measurement up to approximately 49 N, with calibration against a known mass and a calibration factor saved in nonvolatile memory.
  • Live data acquisition in 3 engineering units, with plots over time and CSV export.
  • Propulsion comparison: test results guided the motor and propeller selection for The SMART Hawk.
Planned capability
  • Add 2 force sensors to measure loads in X, Y, and Z.
  • Expand the stand to measure propulsion interactions across 3 axes under asymmetric aircraft wake conditions.
Engineering contribution

Built on the 9th cohort's physical rig and led the instrumentation and software redesign.

Matched the sensor wiring to the firmware, rebuilt the browser interface, and improved ESP32 connection handling. These changes stopped communication drops during testing and enabled continuous data capture, live plots, and timestamped CSV exports.
